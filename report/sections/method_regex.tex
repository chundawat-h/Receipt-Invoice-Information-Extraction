All three methods take the same input, the OCR words of a receipt in
reading order with their bounding boxes, and produce one string per
field.

\subsection{Method 1: OCR + regex baseline}
\label{sec:regex}

The baseline uses no learning (code in \texttt{src/regex\_baseline.py},
notebook \texttt{02\_regex\_baseline.ipynb}). It first rebuilds text
lines from the OCR words: a new line starts when a word's top edge moves
by more than half the previous word's height, or when the next word
jumps back to the left. It then applies one hand-written rule set per
field:

\begin{description}
  \item[Date:] the first match of a set of date patterns, e.g.\
        \texttt{25/12/2018}, \texttt{12-01-19}, \texttt{2018-12-25} and
        \texttt{25 Dec 2018}.
  \item[Total:] amounts (numbers with two decimals) on lines containing
        ``TOTAL'', ``NETT'', ``GRAND'' or ``AMOUNT DUE'', excluding
        subtotal, tax, quantity and change lines, and skipping
        tax-summary rows that hold two different amounts. A total after
        a ``Rounding'' line wins; otherwise lines such as ``Total Incl.\
        GST'' or ``Total Rounded'' are preferred, and a rounding
        adjustment below a plain total is applied. If no such line
        exists, the largest amount on the receipt is used.
  \item[Company:] the first line among the top eight that contains a
        company keyword (\texttt{SDN BHD}, \texttt{ENTERPRISE},
        \texttt{TRADING}, \texttt{RESTAURANT}, \ldots), joined with the
        previous line if it holds only ``SDN BHD'' and with the next one
        if it ends in ``\&''. Store codes and registration numbers at
        either end are removed.
  \item[Address:] the lines after the company, skipping registration
        numbers, up to and including the line with a five-digit
        postcode, plus a short state-name line right after it; phone,
        GST and invoice lines stop the address.
\end{description}

The rules were developed by inspecting errors on the \emph{training}
split only; the test split was used once, for the final score.
