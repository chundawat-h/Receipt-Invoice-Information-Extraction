Scanned receipts are a common business document, yet their key fields
are still often typed in by hand. We study the extraction of four fields
(company, date, address and total) from the SROIE (ICDAR 2019) receipt
dataset and ask how much the 2D position of words on the page adds over
their text alone. On the same OCR output we compare three methods: a
hand-written regular-expression baseline, a text-only BERT token
classifier, and LayoutLM, which adds 2D position embeddings of each
word's bounding box to the BERT input and is otherwise trained
identically. Scored with field-level exact-match $F_1$ on 347 test
receipts, the rules reach a macro $F_1$ of 74.8\%, BERT 91.9\% and
LayoutLM 93.7\%. Layout therefore adds 1.8 points (paired-bootstrap 95\%
CI [0.5, 3.2]). The gain comes from company ($+3.6$) and address
($+2.6$), whose extent on the page is visual, and is zero for the total,
whose training labels often mark a copy of the amount away from the
``TOTAL'' line. Against the official answers, OCR noise caps any tagging
model at 82.8\% macro $F_1$, and LayoutLM comes within 1.2 points of
this ceiling on date and address.
